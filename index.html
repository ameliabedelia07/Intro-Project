About Me & This Project

Positionality Statement

I was a student who had to very heavily consider money when it came to college applications. My parents recently divorced, meaning it wasn’t on the tax forms required for the CSS profile, which most seriously rigorous colleges require. This meant that my family had to write a letter explaining our “unique circumstances”, and the amount I’d pay was left entirely to the discretion of each college’s respective financial aid department rather than the fine tuned algorithms they used for students with representative financial aid documents. I did receive a Pell Grant, placing me within the only 14% of students here at Bryn Mawr College (citing Bryn Mawr’s College Scorecard) whose family’s financial situation qualified for that assistance. I am a member of the STEMLA Fellowship here on campus, a group of limited income students in STEM. In spite of this, I consider myself to be decently well off, and my family never struggled to get our basic needs met. I went to an average public school in an average town, and did not have many responsibilities outside of school that I needed to attend to, meaning I was able to prioritize my education throughout my childhood. My family may not have been in a situation where we could afford for me to apply to college ED, and after getting accepted to colleges, I did have to rule a few out because we simply could not afford them, but I was very fortunate to be able to pay for six or so college applications, and I completed many free applications. Out of everywhere I was admitted, Bryn Mawr did not give me the least costly financial aid, but it was the most affordable of all of the comparable colleges I applied to. All in all, I would never consider myself severely disadvantaged; there are other young adults who are in far less privileged situations than I am.
